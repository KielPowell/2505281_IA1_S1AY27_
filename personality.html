LLMs: A Tool, Not a Crutch

For me, to use large language models like ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot as a tool, not as a crutch means that these technologies could assist me in my learning process, however, they shouldn’t think for me. The process of learning includes more aspects than just providing an answer: I have to learn new concepts, practice some skills and make my conclusions. UNESCO stresses the importance of supporting human agency and questioning the accuracy of information provided by AI (Miao & Holmes, 2023).

I may use LLMs before, during, and after class inside the classroom as long as it is allowed, inside the library, or at home. Before class, I may ask for a simple definition of any new term or ask for some questions to be posed in regards to a reading. When doing the studying, I may ask for practice questions, flashcards, or even a simpler definition of a hard concept. When finishing up the first draft of something I have written, I may ask for critiques or advice on the organization and then choose what to use.

These risks are legitimate. The LLM may provide false or fabricated information in a very convincing way, be biased, or make me want to submit work that I did not even complete myself or comprehend. According to the recommendations of UNESCO, learners should evaluate the output produced by AI and take into account questions of privacy and bias. In any case, I should check facts based on the information provided in the course materials and credible sources, refrain from sharing private information, respect school policies, and report the use of AI where required.

If I use them responsibly, LLMs will be beneficial for my learning process, if not, then using AI will only be detrimental to developing those skills that I need. The best way to evaluate this is by checking if I am still capable of explaining this concept or solving the same problem on my own.
